How Similar Are These Silhouettes? Can We Tell?

❓ The Core Question

When we group 1,833 silhouettes by shape using k-means clustering (k=6), do the resulting clusters represent meaningful visual types? And within each cluster, how many silhouettes are genuinely close to the "average" shape versus genuinely unusual?

We measured each silhouette's Euclidean distance to its cluster center in 946-dimensional space (473 boundary points). Instead of arbitrary quartiles, we used statistical thresholds (mean ± standard deviation) to define four groups:

  • Very Similar (< μ-σ): Within 1 standard deviation below the mean
  • Similar (μ-σ to μ): Between center and 1σ below
  • Unusual (μ to μ+σ): Between center and 1σ above
  • Very Unusual (> μ+σ): Genuinely far from the cluster average

📊 What Do μ and σ Tell Us About Each Cluster?

μ (mean distance): How far, on average, silhouettes in this cluster sit from their cluster center. A higher μ means the cluster's "average shape" is less representative — silhouettes are more spread out from the center.

σ (standard deviation): How much variation exists within the cluster. A low σ means silhouettes are tightly packed around the center (visually consistent); a high σ means the cluster contains diverse shapes.

μ ± σ groups: We split each cluster into four statistical groups: Very Similar (< μ-σ), Similar (μ-σ to μ), Unusual (μ to μ+σ), Very Unusual (> μ+σ). This replaces arbitrary quartiles with statistically meaningful thresholds.
